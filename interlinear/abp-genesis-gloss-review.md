# Genesis 1:1–10 against the Apostolic Bible Polyglot

I checked the preferred English glosses at all 209 Greek word positions in the Athens-LXX interlinear against the Apostolic Bible Polyglot (ABP), reading grouped words as phrases. This review covers the digital ABP Genesis passage and this edition's preferred readings. Alternative glosses and later verses remain outside its scope. The comparison turned up no preferred gloss requiring correction. [ABP Genesis 1](https://biblehub.com/interlinear/apostolic/genesis/1.htm)

| Reference | Finding and editorial decision |
|---|---|
| Genesis 1:1 | Keep “the beginning” for ἀρχῇ in ἐν ἀρχῇ. English article use here is idiomatic. The Greek has no separate article at this position. |
| Genesis 1:2 | Keep “unformed” for ἀκατασκεύαστος. The ABP emphasizes lack of preparation. Both construe the word as a description of the earth. Keep “was moving” for ἐπεφέρετο. The imperfect depicts movement in progress over the water. The lowercase lexical gloss “spirit” leaves capitalization in running English to the translator. |
| Genesis 1:3 | Keep “let there be” and “there came to be.” The first verb commands and the second narrates fulfillment. Both use γίνομαι. |
| Genesis 1:4 | “Saw” and “separated” convey the two actions. Each ἀνὰ μέσον is a single relational expression. Its second token carries a phrase annotation in this interlinear. |
| Genesis 1:5 | Preserve “day one.” The cardinal μία closes the first day, and the ordinal δευτέρα closes the second in 1:8. |
| Genesis 1:6 | Keep “firmament” and “dividing.” This edition has καὶ ἐγένετο οὕτως at positions 19–21. The ABP reading lacks this closing clause. This is a Greek-text difference. |
| Genesis 1:7 | Keep the distinction between water beneath and water above. The second “between” occupies positions 21–22, ἀνὰ μέσον. Positions 29–31 repeat the fulfillment formula at the end of the verse. The ABP ends with “firmament.” Retain the formula here as well as at 1:6. |
| Genesis 1:8 | “Heaven” names the firmament. Both texts include God's judgment of goodness before evening and morning. |
| Genesis 1:9 | Keep “let … be gathered together” for συναχθήτω. The water receives the command to gather. The later noun συναγωγήν echoes the verb. The following command concerns the appearance of dry land. |
| Genesis 1:10 | Keep “collections” for συστήματα. The plural describes the assembled waters, which God names seas. The verse then repeats the judgment of goodness. |

The comparison unit matters. Greek articles can have their own interlinear rows even where English expresses definiteness within a larger phrase. A shared AB-Strong identifier can help locate candidates. The verse, the Greek spelling, and the grammatical construction determine the editorial decision.
